Updates
 

May Workshops — IM Tatev Abrahamyan and GM Josh Friedel are returning to continue their series on endgames and middlegames. IM Kostya Kavutskiy will continue his 1.d4 starter repertoire and calculation classes. GM Jesse Kraai will continue Chernev's book Logical Chess Move by Move.
 
 

Join all classes and learn more here!
 

Blindfold Mates — Do you struggle with visualization? We've released a new blindfold mate in one drill! You'll be prompted with a list of pieces for both sides and need to type in the correct move to checkmate. This drill is very difficult, so if you have trouble, check out the blindfold square color drill we released last month.
 

Game & Profile Review — Interested in having your games and training habits analyzed by one of our senseis? Join the Game & Profile Review! You'll get placed with a cohort of similarly-rated players. Each week, you meet with your peers and analyze one member's game. A few days later, you meet with the sensei, who reviews the same game and answers the questions from your peer review session.  

New Rating Converter — We will soon update our rating converter, which helps standardize Chess.com, Lichess, and OTB players within the Dojo's training program. Check out our video where Jesse dives into the details with Dojoer Vlad Chess, who worked on the statistics for the new conversions. The new conversions should be on the site in about 2 weeks.
 
 
 

Workshops Rebranding
 

We've renamed our Lecture tier to Workshops to better describe the interactive nature of these classes. If you're still on the fence about joining, check out this recommendation from one of our members, Zach:

"I was in the profile and game review group last fall and I did not continue with the lector or review tier options after the new year, for a variety of reasons, but among them was my skepticism that the 'Lectures' would be better than free content I can find online. I am a professor and no one, not the faculty, and CERTAINLY not the students, are excited about lecture format for learning (I say this as one of the better-rated faculty lecturers at my present and former programs). 

Then, a week ago, I saw the free sample recording of one of Sensei Kostya's 'lectures.' It wasn't a lecture at all! It was highly interactive, wherein students discussed homework and brought questions to the sensei. It was more like a grad seminar, or 'workshop' for those outside of academia. That changed everything! I promptly signed up, and I have been enjoying scrambling to catch up with Jesse's Logical Chess workshop, while also working though endgame fundamentals and visualization... how can I find the time for so much great content!? 

Branding is everything, guys, and seeing is believing... Not 'Lecture' - 'Workshop!' Not embargoed, publicize samples! 

Now, is providing these suggestions an act of self sabotage? Part of the reason this is so great is that the classes are small. So, will encouraging more students to sign up ruin it? Or, will it safeguard the project from being abandoned and inspire more content? I hope the latter. Thanks for surprising me!"

— Zach (~1600 Lichess)
